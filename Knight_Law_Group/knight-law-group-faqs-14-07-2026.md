**Knight Law Group \- FAQs**

**Frequently Asked Questions**

1. **Where are you located?**

**A:** We have four offices strategically located across California, allowing us to serve clients throughout the state.

---

2. **What do we do?**

**A:** We help consumers get out of their defective vehicles and get back the money paid in regard to their sales contract/lease agreement. The California Lemon Law makes it favorable to the consumer because it requires the car manufacturer to replace or repurchase your vehicle when they fail to conform it to warranty after a reasonable number of repair attempts.

---

3. **Why should I go with your firm?**

**A:** At Knight Law Group, we get the biggest outcomes. We start where other firms stop, and our clients typically leave here more than 100% whole. Our dedicated team focuses on pursuing the maximum recovery available under California Lemon Law, which may include up to three times the buyback amount, while providing experienced guidance and support throughout the entire process. With a strong track record of holding manufacturers accountable, we are committed to achieving the best possible results for our client

---

4. **Do you file class action lawsuits?**

**A:** We pursue individual claims against car manufacturers. Our goal is to make our client 100% whole again. Typically, class action lawsuits are not favorable to the consumers.

---

5. **I need a new car. Can you help me get a replacement vehicle?**

**A:** The case that we pursue is to get back what you’ve paid. Our goal is to get you out of a defective vehicle and get back the money you’ve paid into the contract.

---

6. **What is a reasonable number of attempts?**

**A:** To be able to assist you, we need to see a history of visits/presentations to the dealership. The California Lemon Law does not specify a number; it only describes a “reasonable” amount. If you’ve had previous visits, we would like to review your repair orders.

---

7. **The dealership does not want to give me my repair documents. Can you request the repair documents on my behalf?**

**A:** If you're unable to obtain the necessary repair documents, we can attempt to request them on your behalf.

---

8. **Why do you need to see my repair documents?**

**A:** The service records are the evidence of repair attempts for your case. They give us a detailed timeline of your dealership visits and the work performed on the vehicle. They document the steps the dealer has taken to address issues, which helps determine how to proceed.

---

10. **What is the fee that I will be charged?**

**A:** The benefit of the California Lemon Law is that the car manufacturer is responsible for paying all attorney’s fees and costs.  You do not send our firm any money or receive a bill from us.

11. **I got an offer from the car manufacturer. Should I accept it?**

**A:** Before you accept an offer, we can review it to determine if they are reimbursing all the money you are owed. For us to review the offer, we will need a copy of your repair orders, a copy of the sales contract/lease agreement, and the actual offer. Once everything is received, we can have an answer typically within (1) business day.

---

12. **How long does it take to review the repair documents?**

**A:** We can review your documents within 24-48 hours. 

---

13. **I think I had a lemon, but I sold the car. Can I still pursue my California Lemon Law rights?**

**A:** It depends on who your car manufacturer is. The CA Lemon Law changed on July 1, 2025, and the car manufacturers that refused to opt-in to the new law are the companies we can still sue on your behalf if you no longer have possession of the vehicle. If your car manufacturer is one that opted into the new law, then unfortunately we cannot help you.

Here is a list of the opt-ins and opt-outs:

**Opted Into the New Law:** Alfa Romeo, Buick, Cadillac, Chevrolet, Chrysler, Dodge, Fiat, Ford, GMC, Hummer, Infiniti, Jaguar, Jeep, Kia, Land Rover, Lincoln, Maserati, Mercedes, Mercury, Mitsubishi, Nissan, Pontiac, RAM, Saturn, Smart, Hyundai, Subaru, Genesis, VinFast

**Not Opted In (Old Law Applies):** Acura, Audi, Bentley, Harley Davidson, Lexus, Mazda, Mini, Porsche, Rolls Royce, Scion, Suzuki, Tesla, Toyota, Volkswagen, Volvo, Honda, BMW, Polestar, McLaren, Fisker

---

14. **Can you recommend me to another firm or lawyer?** 

**A:** Unfortunately, we are unable to provide any referrals. You might consider utilizing search engines like Google or Yelp to seek assistance tailored to your situation.

15. **My car is not paid off. What happens to the amount that I owe?**

**A:** If we can assist, the manufacturer will be responsible for settling the outstanding amount. You would receive a refund of what you’ve paid, while the car manufacturer is responsible for the repayment to the lender.

16. **How long do these cases take?**

**A:** Each situation is unique, and because we are fighting for the biggest outcomes, we want you to prepare for a potentially lengthy process. Typically, these cases can average between 18 to 24 months. The timeline varies widely depending on how far the car manufacturer is willing to go.

17. **Do I continue making my car payments?**

**A:** Yes, you want to continue making payments to your lender. Your lender is not the car manufacturer, and they are not the ones that sold you a defective vehicle. Maintaining regular and up-to-date payments is important, as it establishes a record that reflects your responsible handling of your vehicle-related obligations. 

---

18. **Can I continue driving the car?**

**A:** Yes, throughout the litigation of your case you can continue to drive the vehicle as expected.

---

19. **Would you still be able to help me if I am planning to move to another state in a few months?** 

**A:** If we can proceed with a lawsuit, we absolutely can still help you if you relocate out of California. However, it's important to be willing to travel to California if circumstances require it.

---

20. **I heard I need three repair attempts to qualify.**

**A:** That's not always the case. With the California Lemon Law, what's crucial is a reasonable number of repair attempts. This varies depending on the specific vehicle, the defect in questions and or the repair representations. To determine whether we can move forward, we'll need to review the repair order history.

---

21. **I have repairs, but they were not at the dealership, will that be a problem?**

**A:** If your repair attempts were not at a dealership that is the same brand as your vehicle, we will not be able to help you.

---

22. **I have the repair orders, but they do not state the issues that I brought it in for.**

**A:** i. *If the vehicle is no longer under warranty:* If the repair documents don't clearly state the issues, you brought the vehicle in for, we may be unable to assist.  

*ii. If vehicle still under warranty:* Given that your vehicle is still covered by warranty, I suggest setting up an appointment at your nearest dealership to begin establishing a history of visits.

---

23. **I bought my Tesla/Rivian/Lucid NEW; can you assist me?**

**A.** The Tesla cases we’ve had in the past have gone to arbitration because the consumer, at the time of purchase, agreed to go directly into arbitration with Tesla over any conflict. We do not go to arbitration, we file lawsuits. Therefore, we are unable to assist you.

---

24. **Do you help consumers who have used vehicles?**

Only if the vehicle was sold used CPO (Certified pre-owned) or used – dealer demo. If it was used without being CPO or a dealer demo, no, unfortunately we cannot help.

---

25. **What is a buyback? What is it that you are recovering for me?**

A buyback under California's Lemon Law is when the manufacturer repurchases your defective vehicle. We seek to recover the payments you made toward the purchase or lease of the vehicle, including finance charges paid, sales or use tax, license and initial registration fees, and other official acquisition fees required under your purchase or lease agreement—less the legally required mileage offset for the use of the vehicle before the defect first occurred.

---

26. **What do I need to do during my case? What is my participation?**

Client participation is limited but required throughout the process. You must keep us updated if your contact information changes, provide any documents or information we request, and continue taking your vehicle to the dealership for repairs if problems persist, sending us the repair records from each visit.

Depending on your case you may also be asked to:

* Answer written questions (interrogatories) about your ownership experience.  
* Allow a vehicle inspection. We'll schedule a convenient time, pick up your vehicle, take it to the inspection, and return it the same day. You do not need to be present.  
* Participate in a deposition, where you'll answer questions under oath about your ownership experience. Depositions take place outside of court, and our attorneys will fully prepare you beforehand, either in person or by phone.  
* Attend a court hearing or trial, if necessary. In some cases, you may be required to participate in a Mandatory Settlement Conference (MSC) or a trial. If that happens, our attorneys will prepare you in advance, explain what to expect, and be with you throughout the process.

**Representation Agreement (Retainer) \- FAQs**

1. **What is the purpose of the Representation Agreement?**  
   1. This agreement formally establishes the attorney-client relationship between you and Knight Law Group for a lemon law and/or fraud claim against the vehicle’s manufacturer.

---

2. **When does representation begin?**  
   1. Your representation begins once we receive a signed copy of the agreement.

---

3. **Can I get legal advice before signing?**  
   1. No. Legal advice and attorney-client privilege apply only after the agreement is signed.

---

4. **Do I have to pay upfront legal fees?**  
   1. No. We work on a contingency basis and are only paid if we win or settle your case.

---

5. **Will I owe anything if we don’t win?**  
   1. No.

---

6. **Are there hidden fees or out-of-pocket costs?**  
   1. No. You do not pay anything out of pocket. If we recover “Additional Damages,” those are shared between you and the firm.

---

7. **If I still owe money on the vehicle, what happens?**  
   1. If your case results in a buyback, your remaining loan will be paid off as part of the settlement.

---

8. **Do I keep the vehicle buyback money?**  
   1. Yes. You keep 100% of the buyback amount.

---

9. **What are “Actual Damages”?**  
   1. “Actual Damages” means payments you made towards the purchase/lease of the vehicle itself, finance charges incurred and paid, sales or use tax, license fees and initial registration fees as they appear on the face of the Purchase/Lease Agreement, other official fees you are obligated to pay as part of the initial vehicle acquisition, less the mileage offset for which Client is responsible by law, less the full amount of any negative equity (prior to a reduction by a rebate), less any rebate, and less any amount paid by a third party from disposing of the vehicle (sale, trade-in, repossession auction, insurance payout, or any other means of disposing of the vehicle).

---

10. **What are “Additional Damages”?**  
    1. "Additional Damages" means all damages other than Actual Damages as defined above. Additional Damages may include civil penalties, punitive damages, waiver of mileage offset, recovery of negative equity, towing, out-of-pocket repairs, rental costs, registration other than the initial registration on the purchase/lease contract, insurance premiums, GAP insurance, service contracts, accessories, surface protection products, theft-deterrent devices, recovery of a manufacturer rebate, and prejudgment interest. If awarded, they are split 50/50 between you and the firm.

---

11. **How is the 50% Additional Damages fee calculated?**  
    1. Example:  
       1. Vehicle Buyback \= $10,000 → You keep all $10,000  
       2. Additional Damages \= $20,000 → $10,000 to you, $10,000 to the firm

---

12. **Will I be reimbursed for Uber/rental/towing services?**  
    1. If you incur rental or transportation costs, we will try to get that reimbursed. If you had to have the vehicle towed, we will also try to get you reimbursed for those costs.

---

13. **What is “good mileage”?**  
    1. This refers to the mileage on your vehicle at the time of your first repair attempt. Manufacturers are entitled to deduct these miles from your buyback amount.
